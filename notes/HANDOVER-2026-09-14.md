## 3. The open question -- ANSWERED, and the premise was false

Full write-up: `notes/early-submit-profile.md`. Summary:

**There is no FFN slowdown.** Inside the MoE kernels, across all three arms: launch counts
identical, mean duration within +-1 % (`_moe_up` 3864/3685/3824 us, `_moe_down` 2048/2004/2063 us),
SMs Active 97.9 %, and issue rate, tensor activity, warps in flight and GPC clock all flat. The
32 % was measured on `moe_s`, a **host-side wall clock** around the call -- not a device effect. All
three candidate mechanisms (L2 pollution, controller latency, launch contention) were explanations
of something that does not happen on the GPU.

**The question could not be asked as posed anyway.** There is no memory-bandwidth counter on this
box by any route: `gb20b` has no DRAM metric, `--soc-metrics` answers *"not supported on this
system"*, `dmon`'s `mem %` is stubbed, `ncu` is absent. It was re-posed on counters that exist.

**Early submission works, and cannot matter.** Realised copy/compute overlap goes 3.4 % -> 28.1 %
of copy time (it overlaps attention, never MoE -- `join_pending()` precedes `moe_apply` by
construction, which is correct). But the entire H2D copy time in a 93 s prefill is **2.95 s**.
That is the whole prize for any loader-scheduling change. `all` already hides 1.03 s of it, and
overlapping inflates the copies themselves by 22 %. End to end: 83.0 / 82.1 / 84.7 s -- within
restart noise, unrankable.

So `DSV41_STAGE_BUFS` and read-early/install-late are answers to a **2.8 % question at their
theoretical maximum** and should not be built. The loader-pipeline family is closed -- not by a
hardware wall, but because it optimises 3 % of the wall clock.

**What the profile found instead, and nobody had on a list:**

* the GPU is **idle 55 % of the prefill** (busy 40.3 s of 93.2 s), and H2D explains 3 % of that.
  The remaining ~50 s is host-side: NVMe reads and the work around them.
* `_cb3_unpack_kernel` is the **largest single GPU consumer at 7.44 s**, ahead of `_moe_up_kernel`
  (5.88 s) -- 8 % of the prefill spent unpacking the storage format rather than running the model.
  That is a bigger prize than the entire copy-scheduling question ever was.

## 5. Where the time goes (the map to plan against)

**Decode**, three separate processes: **75–76 % waiting on NVMe, 18 % waiting for the GPU to name
the experts, 1.5 % host bookkeeping.** Everything the model computes is ~5 %.

**Prefill**, per encoder layer under layer-major: **attn+route 53 %, resolve 19 %, FFN 28 %**.

`notes/model-dependency-graph.md` has the model's dependency structure written for someone without
the project's vocabulary, including where the I/O is and where every wait is.

## 6. Method rules this session earned

* **Verify before push.** `ast.parse` is not verification; nor is testing a helper when the call site
  is what changed. Two commits today were reverted or corrected for this. `memory/verify-before-push`.
* **A prefill harness must set `DSV41_PROMPT_CACHE=0`.** Flipping the cache default silently
  invalidated three harnesses in one afternoon, each time making a result look *better*. Varying the
  prompt is not enough — `longctx_profile` builds a longer prompt as an extension of a shorter one, so
  the cache resumes it. Assert on `prompt_cache_reused`.
* **A mechanism is a hypothesis until something measures *it*, not its consequence.** Twice today I
  promoted a plausible mechanism to an explanation — `wait_stream`, refuted by its own diagnostic; then
  "bandwidth", refuted by five minutes of arithmetic. The consequences were solid both times.
* **Three starts, separate processes**, and discard start 1 — it is the cold outlier in every arm.
